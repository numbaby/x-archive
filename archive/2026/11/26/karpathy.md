### 🕐 02:31 UTC · @karpathy

> I asked Claude to populate a Civilization-style game map with real data from the US. Opus 5.5 downloaded and parse a ton of public data and 7 hours and 45K lines of code later... it works :) - 31 real data sources including Census, BEA, BLS, EIA, NOAA, USGS, DoD, NPS, FAA, FEMA, CDC... - Real elevation, land cover, GDP, jobs, power plants, mines, ports, airports, bases, parks, universities, freight flows, disasters; all reconciled onto one 25 km hex grid. - The data is converted into Civilization language, e.g. Gold yield is GDP. A city&apos;s districts are its real industry specializations: San Jose gets a Campus, Detroit is &quot;Motor city&quot;. Its citizens are its real workforce: 1 in 4 San Jose workers are engineers, coders or scientists, against 1 in 14 nationally. Wonders are chosen by published rules, such as national parks by visitor numbers and universities by R&amp;D spending, etc etc. - 19 lenses (income, degrees, faith, disasters…), a City View for 524 metros, and nine advisors plus an Explorer. The Explorer turns any column for any of 3,100+ counties into a table and scatter plot, linked to the map. - Claude decided to skip a few states and territories sry - Built using Claude Code desktop app, Opus 5.5 on medium and turning on ultracode. It&apos;s deployed here: https://karpathy.ai/usaciv/ and the &quot;?&quot; has the source link too. I don&apos;t exactly know where I was going with this but it&apos;s really fun to browse and play with. Possibly it can be expanded or built into a game.
