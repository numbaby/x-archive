### 🕐 02:40 UTC · @ylecun

> JEPA has been something we follow very closely, and over the last couple of weeks, @ylecun and his collaborators at AMI Labs, NYU, and Brown have made huge progress with it. I reached out to @randall_balestr , who co-authored &quot;Counterfactual World Models&quot;, to ask what he found most interesting in the paper. He told me that touch might be the frontier of what offline data can teach a robot. A video of a hand barely missing a mug doesn&apos;t tell you what would&apos;ve happened if it had touched it. Maybe robots need to try things for themselves to learn. That conversation inspired me to make this video. I walk through two papers in detail (but keep it accessible): 1. The Content–Geometry Tradeoff in World Models: A JEPA can learn the layout of a city walk from first-person views and movement commands, without GPS! But in a separate experiment, it can lose track of a cube sitting right in front of it. The proposed fix, Slow SVReg, helps the model hold on to details that don’t change much from one frame to the next. 2. JEPAs that ask “What if?” (Counterfactual World Models) Say a current pushed your boat off course. Where would you have ended up if you’d steered left in that same current? With residual reuse, you take the gap between where you landed and where JEPA predicted you’d land, then add it to the prediction for steering left. Watch this video explainer to see the whole picture with demos, visuals, and Randall&apos;s full note to get a feel for it. Also, on Youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oaIgPi4OLcU Media
