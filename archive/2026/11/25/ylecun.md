### 🕐 17:32 UTC · @ylecun

> This new essay by Steven Pinker is so illuminating that I don’t know where to begin. Let me start with intelligence. The whole debate about human-level artificial intelligence assumes that intelligence is some unidimensional universal construct. But this is not true. Even if IQ captures differences within our species, this does not work across species. A cat can solve in a split second problems that we struggle with. With machines, it’s even clearer. A boat is more powerful than a human. But it will never climb the stairs. The problem with treating intelligence as a unidimensional, universal construct is that it generates extreme scenarios: Once “intelligence” gets high enough, AI will either destroy humanity or solve all its problems. But this ignores bodies. Infrastructure. Physical limits. In reality, AI will probably solve some problems and create others. We should be working together to reduce the harms and increase the benefits, without falling prey of catastrophic scenarios. And now the most unsettling part. In some circles is emerging the idea that machines may become conscious and that that we should also protect machines from us. Suppose you give a chatbot the tiniest moral weight because there’s a chance it might suffer. If you add up those weights, eventually saving enough chatbots could justify killing a person. The idea of conscious machines is extremely dangerous. We should build machines that help us our problems, not machines that are conscious and create even more problems. * Full essay in the first comment.
