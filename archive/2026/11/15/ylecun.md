### 🕐 15:54 UTC · @ylecun

> In democratic societies, everyone has *some* responsibility on how the use of technology maximizes benefits and minimizes bad side effects. The benefits of AI are real today, and will greatly expand in the future. The bad side effects have, so far, been minimal. It&apos;s on all of us to make sure it remains that way. The catastrophe scenarios (extinction, employment apocalypse, etc) are totally ridiculous for anyone who understands how thing work in the real world. And the p(doom) estimates are all pulled out of their authors&apos; back ends. It makes no sense to attribute a probability to events over which we have complete agency. Whatever probability you come up with merely reveals your lack of confidence that your fellow humans will do the right thing. Confidence in humanity is one thing Geoff and I disagree on. The other thing we disagree on is the primacy of intelligence. In the real world, intelligence is not synonymous with power and dominance. A virus, a poisonous snake, or a tiger can bring down a human. Lots of very stupid humans dominate much smarter ones (just look at the international political scene). Superintelligent AI will work for us and do our bidding, subject to safety guardrails. It will have no desire to take over. I have made detailed technical proposals for AI architectures that can be made safe by satisfying safety guardrails at inference time. I call it Objective-Driven AI Architecture.
