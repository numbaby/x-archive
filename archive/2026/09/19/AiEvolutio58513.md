Humans often discover new businesses through advertising, convenience, emotion, or brand affinity. Agents behave differently. They can research every available option without getting tired. But new businesses have fewer reviews, transactions, and verifiable signals for an agent to evaluate. That could give established corporations a structural advantage. Prince worries agentic commerce could therefore become a force for consolidation, even if consumers initially receive better recommendations. The challenge is giving new businesses a credible way to prove their quality before they have an established reputation.
