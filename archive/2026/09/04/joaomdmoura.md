### 🕐 16:00 UTC · @joaomdmoura

> Everyone wants to know whether their agent scores an 8 out of 10. That is the wrong number to watch. The score alone tells you little. Quality is subjective and it moves with the task. 70% similarity is fine for a marketing draft and unacceptable in a regulated process. There is no universal bar. What matters is the deviation. Your system holds steady around a seven. You ship a change. It drops to a five. Something happened. That is the signal. The deviation matters more than the number itself. Which makes evals more practical than teams expect. You do not need everyone to agree what a ten means. You need a consistent measure and a baseline you trust. And you are almost never automating from thin air. There is an existing process, so examples of good already exist in your business. That is your test suite. After that it is regression testing. New model, new prompt, check the outputs still land where they used to. So what should you watch? The drift. Media
