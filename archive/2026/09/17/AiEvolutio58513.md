> Marc Andreessen argues that raw intelligence can be a poor leadership trait, and it changes how we should think about AI... “If the leader is more than one standard deviation of IQ away from the followers, it’s a real problem.” He points to the U.S. military, an early adopter of IQ testing. Over time, they observed a consistent pattern. Leaders far below their teams cannot model how their people think, but leaders far above them struggle as well. “It’s actually very hard for very smart people to model the internal thought processes of even moderately smart people.” When the IQ gap gets too wide, leaders lose theory of mind. Push it further and you get his AI conclusion: “If you had a person or a machine… with a thousand IQ… its understanding of reality would be so alien… it wouldn’t even be able to connect.” A superintelligent AI would not be well suited to lead humans because it might be unintelligible to them. Leadership is not an intelligence problem. It is a connection problem. Past a point, more IQ only widens the gap. The world will not be run by the smartest thing in the room for a long time, maybe ever. Media
