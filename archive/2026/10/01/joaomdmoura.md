### 🕐 16:00 UTC · @joaomdmoura

> We built a feature that gave customers access to 2,000 tool integrations. They told us they wanted 20. We had watched MCP get adopted everywhere and built something around it. Thousands of servers ready to connect. I thought the value was obvious. Then we took it to customers, and that was not what they wanted. They said, well, that is great, but we actually have 20 internal MCPs and we want to use those. Not a public catalog. A private registry of what their own teams had built. Funnily enough, I got it wrong for a simple reason. I was thinking like someone building a platform instead of someone running an enterprise. From outside, more connectors reads as more capability. From inside, what matters is what is already wired in, with your auth around it and your data rules on top. The public ones are a convenience. The internal ones are the business. So what are enterprises buying when they insist on standards? Not access to everything. The right to change their minds later. Media
